Central AI team · briefing

fleetopt

It checks the AI agents our teams build, finds where they waste money or are more complicated than the job needs, fixes what it can prove, and leaves the decision to the team.

fleetopt apply <the team's project>

One command. It runs from start to finish without anyone watching.

Why it exists

Every agent costs money each time it runs.Teams across the company build them. Small waste in one agent becomes a large bill across hundreds.

Many are built bigger than the job.Several agents talking to each other where one would do. Steps that never change the answer.

Nobody can review them all by hand.A central team can look closely at a few. It cannot look at thousands.

How it works

Seven steps, in order, with no one driving

  1. Start

    Finds the agent in the team's project and checks it can run. If something is missing, it lists what and stops.

  2. Watch

    Runs the agent on the team's own test questions and records every step. The team's code is not touched.

  3. Review

    Reports where money is wasted and whether the design fits the job. Gives the agent a level from 0 to 4.

  4. Change

    Makes one change at a time, on a separate copy of the code.

  5. Prove

    Runs the agent again and checks the answers. A change that does not hold up is undone.

  6. Repeat

    Looks again. One fix often uncovers the next.

  7. Report

    Says what changed, what it gained, and whether that is proven.

A team that only wants to look can stop after step 3. Nothing is changed.

The level

One number says how far an agent is from where it should be

  • 0

    Fit

    Nothing worth changing.

  • 1

    Wasteful

    Works, and costs more than it should.

  • 2

    Over-built

    Parts of the design do nothing.

  • 3

    Wrong shape

    A simpler design would do the whole job.

  • 4

    Broken

    Requests fail today.

Across many agents the levels become the report: how many are fit, how many are wasteful, how many are broken, and what each group costs.

What keeps it safe

Rules it cannot break, rather than promises

  • The team's code is never edited in place. Every change goes to a separate copy.
  • Nothing is merged or published. A person on the team decides.
  • A change is kept only if it is measured to help and the answers are still right.
  • A change to the design is tried only when the team has test questions with known right answers.
  • The verdict is calculated from the measurements. The AI that made the change does not grade itself.
  • It installs nothing on a developer's machine and never reads passwords or keys.
The exchange

What a team provides

  • A project that already runs
  • Test questions with the right answers, if they want design changes

What a team gets back

  • A review, with evidence for every finding
  • The proven changes, ready for them to accept or drop
  • A short summary, and a full record for audit

Where it stands · 28 September 2026

What has been shown, and what has not

No change has yet been kept on a real agent. It has found real problems every time and has refused every change it could not prove.

Shown on real agents

  • Started 3 of 3 agents with no help from their authors
  • Found real problems in all 3, including one where no request finishes
  • Built a redesign on its own, measured it 23% faster, and rejected it for wrong answers
  • Took a broken agent from 0 of 6 requests finished to 4 of 6
  • Every verdict it gave was true

Not yet shown

  • A change kept on a real agent
  • A run cheap enough for thousands of agents. Today a full run costs about $3 to $4
  • One report across many agents
  • Agents built with tools other than LangGraph
  • AI providers other than Anthropic
What comes next
  1. Prove a kept improvement. Run it on more real agents until changes stand.
  2. Bring the cost of a run down. Skip findings too small to matter.
  3. One report across the fleet. Every agent, its level, its cost.
  4. More kinds of agent. Other frameworks and other AI providers.
